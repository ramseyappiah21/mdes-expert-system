The problem

Students and advisors must apply a dense web of prerequisites, credit limits, standing bands, and milestone gates.

Local question

May I take CE301 this semester?

Global question

Given this transcript and CGPA, what is the safest next step?

Both questions are logical. Both are easy to get wrong in conversation alone.

Problem definition2 / 14

Domain and user

Target user: a CSE undergraduate, or an advisor acting for that student.

  • Which courses are legal next semester — and why?
  • What credit load is safe on this standing?
  • Internship, Final Year Project, graduation: open or closed?
  • Which electives and career track match declared interests?

Chosen because the lecture examples are already student/course/eligibility triples, and because prerequisite graphs demand recursive reasoning.

Checkpoint: domain justification3 / 14

Architecture

1. Consultation

Context questions, validation, guest session facts.

2. Inference

Backward, forward, and recursive engines.

3. Knowledge base

529 facts · 50 named rules · 43-course catalogue.

4. Explanation

Satisfied conditions printed as why / why-not.

The interface never invents a recommendation. It asserts facts, then asks the same goals a programmer would type at the prompt.

Knowledge-base design5 / 14

Rule groups (50 named rules)

R01–R11

Prerequisites, offerings, eligibility.

R12–R23

GPA, standing, cores, electives, catch-up.

R24–R34

Interest match, scoring, load, semester pack.

R35–R50

Internship, FYP, graduation, what-if, support.

Eligibility is universal quantification over direct prerequisites. Ancestor recursion is reserved for explanation and “how far back is the blockage?”

Logical rule design6 / 14

Three reasoning modes

Backward — menu 3

eligible_for(yaw, ce301) starts from a goal and proves or fails. Natural when the user already has a yes/no question.

Forward — menu 5

From Kofi’s facts the engine pushes warning, reduced load, and support actions. Kofi never asked “am I at risk?”

Recursive — menu 4

ancestor_prerequisite(ce407, ce101) walks Final Year Project II back to Introduction to Computing.

Checkpoint: backward vs forward7 / 14

Interaction and explanation

Context questions

Year 1 is not asked about FYP. CGPA < 2.00 triggers recovery questions. AI + year 3 asks about MA202.

Why / why-not

Each course card lists satisfied rules: core, interest, gating value, level fit. Refusals list the missing chain.

Explanations are re-queried from the same rules that fired. Change a prerequisite and both the decision and the sentence change.

Checkpoint: explanation design8 / 14

Error handling

  • Unknown tokens: maybe, skip, unknown
  • Invalid year or menu index is rejected and repeated
  • Unknown course codes cannot enter the catalogue
  • Missing CGPA defaults to a conservative 2.50, not 4.00
  • quit cancels; unexpected errors restart the menu

Testing strategy11 / 14

Strengths and limits

Strengths

One KB, three inference styles. Derived explanations. Standing-aware load. Demo oracles built into the facts.

Limits

Teaching curriculum, not the official handbook. No timetable clashes. Simple (not credit-weighted) GPA. Single session.
